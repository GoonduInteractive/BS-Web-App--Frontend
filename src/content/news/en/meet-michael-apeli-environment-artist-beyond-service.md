### **Meet Michael Apeli, the Artist Building the World of Beyond Service**

Every fight in Beyond Service happens somewhere. The ground under the fighters, the rock around them and the light across the arena all had to be designed and built. At Goondu Games, that work is led by Michael Apeli, Lead Technical Environment Artist.

[Watch Michael's interview on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DgGT4sZNFaH/)

### **Building the world the characters live in**

"My job is basically the environment where the characters inhabit," Michael says. "It's making sure it's aesthetically pleasing to players."

A technical environment artist works between art and engineering. Michael sets the visual direction for the game's environments, plans how each one is put together, and builds the workflows that get them running inside the game. Beyond Service launches on mobile first, so every environment has to look right and still run well on a phone.

### **From mechanical engineering to game worlds**

Michael did not start in games. He studied Mechanical Engineering at Niger Delta University and completed his industrial training at an auto engineering workshop in Yenagoa, Bayelsa State.

His National Youth Service year took him to Quiva Games in Enugu, where he supported game art production. He stayed on for nearly three years as a Technical Environment Artist, building mobile games and focusing on visual design, asset creation and environment detail. He joined Goondu Games in October 2025.

### **An artist before the job title**

Michael was making art long before it was his job. He draws, and he sculpts figures out of dry paper. Both, he says, come naturally to him. Outside the studio, he follows astronomy and theoretical physics, and he loves theatre.

His ArtStation portfolio shows the technical side of that craft: procedural clouds built in Blender, a procedural brick and plaster material, terrain studies, foliage packs, a free skybox collection for other artists, and Edge of Infinity, a portfolio piece he has reworked through seven iterations.

[See Michael's work on ArtStation](https://pelitonic.artstation.com/)

### **Something that belongs to us**

For Michael, the point of Beyond Service is ownership. The game is rooted in African mythology, and the team built its world from that culture rather than borrowing someone else's.

"We created something cultural, something to create a sense of belonging," he says. "Something that belongs to us. We're not co-opting anybody's culture. We're not co-opting anybody's IP. Something original."

### **What players should expect**

Michael's goal for every environment is that players remember it after they close the game.

"Expect something fun, something catching, something that will steal your attention," he says. "You find yourself randomly thinking about it every day, and it gives you a sense of awe and wonder."

### **Three years from now**

Michael sees Beyond Service growing well beyond its first release. "In three years, I see us building this out into a very sizeable franchise," he says. "Something that has become a household name."

That growth starts with a world players want to come back to, and that is the part Michael builds.

### **FAQ**

**Who is Michael Apeli?**

Michael Apeli is the Lead Technical Environment Artist at Goondu Games. He leads environment art and design for Beyond Service.

**What does a technical environment artist do?**

A technical environment artist designs and builds the worlds of a game and makes sure they run well inside the game engine. On Beyond Service, that includes setting the visual direction for environments and the workflows used to build them.

**Is Beyond Service an original African game?**

Yes. Beyond Service is an original game rooted in African mythology, built by Goondu Games in Enugu, Nigeria.
